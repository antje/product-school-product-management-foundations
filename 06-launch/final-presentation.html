Slide 5 · Strategy · Modules 1 and 3

Coordinators drop off at compliance logging, so we fix that step first

Problem hook

We must keep our largest account and win back daily use by making frontline work faster in RouteLogic than texts, WhatsApp and paper.

Value proposition

For fleet coordinators and their drivers, we will make daily work faster than texts, WhatsApp and paper, because our largest account is deciding during its trial. The biggest gain is compliance logging, the slowest step. Features the frontline never uses move one step away, for the managers who bought them.

Hypothesis

Based on dispatchers who "can't trust the board" and 14.6-minute compliance logs, I believe faster compliance logging will get fleet coordinators to finish their day in RouteLogic. It will show as a 25% increase in compliance-step completion.

Slide 8 · Validation · Module 5

A 14-day A/B test decides whether B1 goes to every account

Hypothesis · VEL-01

I believe that B1, the One-Click Compliance Checklist, for fleet coordinators will result in more compliance logged in RouteLogic, as measured by a +12-point change in daily compliance-step completion within 14 days.

Control (A)

Today's compliance step: several screens, details re-typed, 14.6 minutes.

Variant (B)

The B1 checklist, behind a feature flag tied to the login.

Baseline
48%
MDE
+12 pts
Per arm
270
Read
Day 14
Guardrails, against control

Record accuracy: corrected or flagged records no more than 2 points above control. Fail rate: not below half of control, because a collapse means Pass is tapped without checking.

Decision rules
Ship

+12 points or more at p < 0.05, both guardrails held, same direction in 2 of 3 accounts, and the week-2 lift at least half of week 1.

Iterate

Positive but below 12 points, or a confidence interval that crosses zero.

Investigate

The bar is cleared but a guardrail breaks, or the accounts disagree.

Kill

No improvement, or record accuracy breached by more than twice its boundary.

Why this design

About 540 coordinators sit below the usual 1,000-user guide for an A/B test. Measuring each coordinator's daily rate gives about ten observations per person. A canary goes first, and the flag allows instant rollback.

Bad signal

Coordinators open B1 but don't submit. A wave below 85% first-week activation holds the next wave until the cause is known.

Also watched: completion rises while the Fail rate collapses, which means Pass is tapped without checking.

Scariest assumption

Every account can use the same 12 checks. The CS lead checks each account's list by week 3, before any wave is set.

Most likely decision · Iterate

If waves with different checklists stall below 60% while matching accounts clear it, B1 stays on where it fits and the rest wait for a checklist per account.

Slide 10 · Story · Individual insights

The research told me where people complain. The data told me where the workflow breaks.

Aha moment
4.3
DRIVERS × DISPATCH
2.2
COORDINATORS × COMPLIANCE

I went looking for the heatmap cell that matched my driver's misery, and it scored 4.3. The lowest score sat one row over: coordinators rating a feature they use every day at 2.2.

Friction points
  • Letting go of the persona after two modules of driver research.
  • Writing a guardrail that can fail. "No incomplete records" was impossible once submit was blocked.
  • Planning a launch before the result exists. A cold pressure test caught an email that quoted a result a week early.
Key learnings
  • The Now lane holds one item, and six features went to the cut list.
  • My instincts needed the same audit as the AI's scores.
  • A release is not a launch. Go-to-market amplifies a proven result.
  • B2B changes the experiment: the per-coordinator daily rate makes a small pilot readable.
Not yet proven

VEL-01 hasn't run, so B1's lift is still a hypothesis. Whether every account can use the same 12 checks is unknown. The renewal also depends on the route-change and crash bugs that B1 doesn't touch.
