# Problem Hook & Value Proposition: RouteLogic Velocity

Module 1 · Deliverable 1. The discovery groundwork behind it is in [`strategic-map.md`](strategic-map.md).

## 1. Chosen scenario

**Path:** RouteLogic Velocity (B2B)

The risk is concrete and close: our largest enterprise account is already trialing a leaner competitor, and the people who run fleets every day are using RouteLogic less.

## 2. The strategic crisis

> If we do nothing for 12 months, the company will lose the daily user while still winning the executive demo. Coordinator usage is already falling across the base, and our largest account is trialing a leaner rival because RouteLogic slows its most time-sensitive work. That trial is the first visible exit, not the only one. As coordination moves to phones, spreadsheets and lighter tools, RouteLogic becomes the back office admins log into for reports. A reporting layer on top of work done elsewhere is easy to replace. "The most powerful operating system" becomes "the expensive back office."

## 3. The moment of misery

> The user is currently forced to work around RouteLogic at the moments that matter most. A driver calls in late and a route has to be reassigned before the trucks leave. RouteLogic can do it, but the steps sit behind layers built for compliance and finance. So the coordinator solves it by phone, in a group text with drivers and on a spreadsheet, then types the result into RouteLogic later, if at all. The system of record drifts away from what actually happened on the road.

## 4. Problem hook

> We must keep our largest account and reverse the drop in daily use across our base by making coordinators' most time-sensitive work faster inside RouteLogic than on the phone and spreadsheet they fall back on today.

## 5. Value proposition

> For **frontline fleet coordinators, the 95% who rely on RouteLogic every day**, we will **make assign, reroute and track faster than the phone and spreadsheet they fall back on today, by moving the compliance and finance layers off their screens while keeping them one step away for the admins and executives who bought RouteLogic for them**, because **our largest account is deciding during its trial and coordinator usage is already falling across the base**.

## 6. Cold-read self-review

I ran the cold read with a skeptical VP of Finance who saw only the hook and value proposition. The verdict on the first draft: "high enough to get a meeting, not to get funding." The answers below record what changed.

| Question | Your answer |
|---|---|
| Is the business risk high-stakes enough to justify a new initiative? | A high-stakes threat, but not yet a funded one. My first draft rested on one account and the claim that other fleets would follow, which nothing supported. The hook now rests on the broad usage decline the brief reports. To fund it, I still need the account's revenue and decision date, a cost, and an answer to whether anything can ship before the trial ends. |
| Is the moment of misery systemic, or just an edge case? | Systemic. The brief reports a sharp decline in usage among coordinators as a group, and says the extra layers turned a simple job into a struggle for the 95% who rely on RouteLogic daily. These are the core users. The cold read caught that my first draft hid this and read like a project to save one deal, so the hook and value proposition now say it outright. Still to prove: the exact workaround (phone, group text, spreadsheet) is my inference, and Module 2 research has to confirm it. |
| Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle? | It removes it, as long as the obstacle is the layers. The brief blames the layers ("administrative burden", "extra layers"), and the value proposition names the three jobs it speeds up. Two fixes came from the cold read. The bar is now "faster than the phone and spreadsheet", not "fastest path in the product", because the fastest path inside a slow product can still lose to a phone. And the layers stay one step away for the executives who bought RouteLogic for them, so the buyer loses nothing. It would not help if the slowness is the software itself, such as load times, so I need a time baseline for assign and reroute before building. |
