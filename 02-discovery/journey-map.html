RouteLogic VelocityCoordinator compliance journey

Log compliance from the assigned route, with every check answered.

B1 would reuse route details, collect each checklist answer and confirm the saved record. The pilot tests whether coordinators complete more of their required daily logs.

Who: a fleet coordinator who assigns routes, logs compliance and hands off shifts.

Goal: finish the compliance record and return to dispatch, with the result visible.

14.6 min mean logging time in the scenario, against a 3-min benchmark.

71% assign routes; 48% log compliance. The funnel identifies the step to investigate.

Proposed workflow. No interview describes the compliance flow. Observe it before build.

  1. 1

    Assign the route

    Assign a driver and vehicle → open compliance from the route.

    Internal state
    I know which route this record belongs to.
    Friction addressed
    The checklist entry stays beside the route the coordinator just assigned.

    Boundary: a route missing a driver or vehicle stays blocked until assignment is fixed.

  2. 2

    Check the header

    Inspect six read-only route fields → avoid copying data into the log.

    Internal state
    I can see where these details came from.
    Friction addressed
    Reuse route, driver, vehicle, date, shift and coordinator data already held in RouteLogic.

    Recovery: wrong details go back to route assignment; B1 does not guess or edit them.

  3. 3

    Answer the checks

    Answer 12 blank checks; explain each Fail → record a complete response.

    Internal state
    I know what I answered and what still needs attention.
    Friction addressed
    One screen, one tap per Pass. A Fail needs a reason; “Other” needs a note.

    Boundary: nothing is pre-answered. A tap records an answer, not proof the check happened.

  4. 4

    Submit and confirm

    Submit once and read confirmation → know the log was recorded.

    Internal state
    I can return to dispatch with the result visible.
    Friction addressed
    The confirmation shows passes, fails and reasons. Each route and shift gets one record.

    Recovery: corrections use the agreed audit path, with the existing record format preserved.

Three advantages to validate against today's logging workflow

Reuse the route headerThe proposed log needs no retyping of its six header fields. Observe today's workflow to measure how much copying this removes.

Require explicit answersEach check starts blank and needs a response. Audits still need to test whether the answers reflect the checks performed.

Keep the buyer's recordPreserve the existing record format and correction path, subject to the compliance owner's sign-off before the pilot.

Before build week 1: observe compliance logging at each pilot account. Confirm the 12 checks, available route fields, correction path and time spent checking versus entering data. Current screens and repeated entry remain hypotheses. B1 does not fix driver delivery confirmation, photo uploads, offline routes, reassignment notifications or live two-way sync.

Evidence: Module 3 scenario funnel, mean time and CSAT; UXR-02 / UXR-09 establish broader coordination workarounds, not off-platform compliance logging.

Research and assumptions · B1 requirements

Earlier driver journey, retained as the Module 2 baseline

Before the Module 3 pivot: the persona was a delivery driver making dozens of stops a day, often at a doorstep with one hand free. The observed friction was three screens to mark a stop delivered, leading one driver to text the dispatcher instead (UXR-01, BUG-2055). Five of seven focus-group drivers carried paper manifests (UXR-12), and UXR-06 described a morning screenshot.

  1. Start route: a simplified home screen and a cached route were proposed to replace menu hunting and backup copies.
  2. Arrive at stop: opening the current stop was proposed to reduce phone handling.
  3. Confirm delivery: one-tap delivery and clear photo confirmation were proposed instead of a text message.
  4. Move to the next stop: reassignment alerts and fresh driver status were proposed instead of relying on WhatsApp.

The original advantages were one action creating one record, richer structured stop data and updates in both directions. They were broader Velocity aspirations, not measured outcomes or B1 capabilities. Texts can carry identifiers and photos; they do not enforce the structured record RouteLogic needs.

Module 3 shifted the priority to coordinator compliance: its 2.2 CSAT and the largest mean time gap supported the next test. The driver's documented failures remain real; the B1 pilot simply does not claim to resolve them.
